−600 m · the fleet

Who else can read your tables?

Plenty of tools are open source. Plenty are encrypted. The question that matters is narrower: when your data sits on a server, can the operator read it? Here is the honest answer for the nearest alternatives.

Tool Server can read your data How it syncs Relational tables Formulas Licence
TideWork No — it only ever holds ciphertext Any Matrix homeserver — ours or yours Yes Yes Apache-2.0
Airtable Yes Airtable's cloud, and only theirs Yes Yes Proprietary
Anytype No — end-to-end encrypted Peer-to-peer, relayed by its own network Linked objects, multiple views No Source-available (its own licence)
CryptPad No — end-to-end encrypted Any CryptPad instance Spreadsheets, not linked tables Spreadsheet formulas AGPL-3.0
Grist Yes Their cloud, or self-hosted Yes Yes (Python) Apache-2.0
Baserow Yes Their cloud, or self-hosted Yes Yes MIT / premium
NocoDB Yes Their cloud, or self-hosted Yes Yes AGPL-3.0

“Server can read your data” means the operator of the server holding your data can see its contents — not whether disks are encrypted at rest, which is true of nearly everyone and protects against a stolen disk rather than against the operator. Self-hosting any of the bottom four makes you that operator, which is a real answer — it is just a different one from “nobody can.”

Anytype is the closest thing to this, and genuinely end-to-end encrypted; it publishes its source under its own Any Source Available License rather than an OSI-approved one, and has linked objects and views but no formulas. It also syncs differently: devices talk to each other peer-to-peer, relayed through Anytype’s own network, rather than through a server you choose — so “host it yourself” means running their network nodes, not pointing the app at a server you already have. CryptPad’s Sheet is a real encrypted spreadsheet — spreadsheets and linked tables are different tools. Corrections welcome: tell us and we will fix this table.
